Three calculations that turn the records into decisions: where learners are heading, whether marking can be done in time, and where limited equipment should go.
Projected outcomes
Loading…
Method. A least-squares regression is fitted to each learner's marks in the order they were awarded. The line is projected two assessments forward, then shrunk towards the learner's current average in proportion to how little evidence supports it — two marks on a steep slope are not extrapolated as confidently as six on a clear line. The projection is combined with attendance (0.35) and submission rate (0.20) into a completion probability.

Marking workload
Loading…
Method. Outstanding submissions are ordered Earliest Deadline First, which is the optimal ordering for meeting deadlines with one marker — if any order can meet them all, this one does. Work is laid out day by day against the assumed capacity. The schedule then reports the minimum daily capacity that would clear every remaining deadline, so a manager knows what help is needed rather than only that there is a problem.

Equipment allocation
Loading…
Method. Each school is scored on unmet need (0.55), attendance (0.25) and retention risk (0.20). Each additional device at a school is worth slightly less than the one before, so the benefit function is concave — which means repeatedly giving the next device wherever it gains the most produces the highest total benefit achievable. The result is compared against splitting the batch evenly, which is what would otherwise happen.
